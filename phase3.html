Research Validation · Phase 1

The science
is clear.

Expert interview with Mickaël Grévois (15+ reptiles, certified breeder) confirmed the core hypothesis: behavioral self-regulation in reptile handling maps directly to autism therapy.

🧬
Oxytocin release confirmed
Reptile interaction triggers oxytocin — the bonding hormone — even without vocalisation or eye contact, making it uniquely effective for ASD Level 1.
🦎
Behavioral self-regulation mechanism
Reptiles require calm, slow movement from handlers. This constraint becomes the therapy: the animal teaches regulation.
📊
Gap confirmed: no shared data standard
No system exists for breeders/therapists to monitor, share, or benchmark reptile care data across clinical settings.
"Reptiles require behavioral self-regulation from handlers. That constraint is not a barrier — it is the therapy itself."

Mickaël Grévois · Technicien en métrologie · Éleveur capacitaire
