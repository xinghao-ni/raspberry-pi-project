01

The Gecko That Started Everything

In high school I kept a gecko. Due to inexperience, I didn't prepare a heating mat in winter. By the time I realized, it had already died and begun to decompose. This made me truly understand for the first time that there is a great distance between loving animals and being capable of caring for them.

Between 2019 and 2023, I lost my grandmother, grandfather, great-grandmother, and my 12-year companion dog in succession. Those years made me face death very directly. I realized that the images I truly leave behind are very few — many memories distort, blur, and eventually disappear. From then on I started a documentary studio, hoping to preserve through film those people, relationships, and feelings that will eventually vanish.

02

The Pivot — From Hardware to System

Early in the project, I focused almost entirely on "how to sell this smart tank" — functionality, marketing, cost, market acceptance. In France, I read research about bearded dragons being used in emotional support and with special populations, and spoke with relevant families and specialists.

At that moment I suddenly realized my work shouldn't stay at the pet equipment level. Reptile value, the meaning of environmental control, the direction of interactive design — everything had to be redefined.

I decided to actively overturn the original path — from a single piece of smart hardware, to a system built around animal state, user emotion, AI recognition, and special scenario support. This decision meant higher uncertainty, and meant stepping outside the market logic I was already familiar with. But I still chose the harder road, because I know the things truly worth doing are almost always found outside the industry's default answers.

03

On Fear & Free Fall

In Portugal I learned skydiving, and in Marrakech I completed cliff diving. The moment the plane door opened, cold air rushed in violently; adrenaline surged instantly, the whole body pushed into a state of extreme alertness. The true moment of jumping — fear, weightlessness, air pressure and heartbeat arrived simultaneously. The body seemed to suddenly detach from familiar order; ears filled only with the sound of wind; consciousness gathered with unprecedented focus.

That kind of pain was very real, also very direct — like life itself reminding you of its existence in the most primal way.

These experiences let me more deeply feel the intensity, freedom, and weight of life. I began to more seriously understand the meaning of sensation, fear, pain, excitement, and courage in a person. I learned to slowly release from others' expectations, and rebuild the relationship with myself and the world.

04

2011 — The Accident

In 2011, I was in the most serious car accident of my life. It was dusk, no street lights, no phone nearby, visibility and judgment severely limited. An elderly person on an electric bike was hit — they suffered a skull fracture; my own nasal bridge broke on the spot. The moment I picked myself up, I was completely dazed, couldn't believe what had just happened was real. Fear, shock, pain, and emptiness pressed down simultaneously — I even flashed on a very instinctive thought: run.

But roughly two minutes later, I forced myself to calm down and began first aid. Called for help, contacted family — one hand still on my fractured, bleeding nose, still observing the victim's condition. My father stayed in the hospital ward all night. The elderly person survived.

From then on, I gradually formed an ability: when facing pressure, first stabilize myself, then handle the problem. I know how to quickly identify what matters most in chaos.
05

Special Skill — Fine Sensitivity

My advantage comes from a very fine sensitivity. I am acutely sensitive to color, light, materials, tone, expressions, pauses, spatial order, and emotional flow — things many people pass over in a glance stay with me longer, more clearly. I gradually realized I may carry some characteristics of Type 1 autism spectrum, which keeps my perception permanently open, and also makes it easier for me to enter deeply into other people's emotions — to hear the parts that haven't yet been organized into language.

Many people come to me not only for aesthetic judgment or brand expression. They are more seeking a sense of being understood. My ability is to recognize the true core from chaos — to slowly smooth out scattered emotions, sensations, and information, and return them to a place that has order and warmth.

Many people feel I live very freely, as if I carry no pressure. But that ease comes from having passed through fear, self-doubt, and inner struggle one time after another — and what remains is clarity and honesty.
06

Role — Guardian of the Concept

I am the project founder, responsible for overall direction, product definition, research advancement, and external communication. In the team I am more the guardian of this project's concept and temperament.

The most important foundation of the company is respect for life and people. This project carries a finer, more lasting relationship — one that makes people willing to spend time observing life, feeling life, and in that relationship build connection and a sense of responsibility.

My role is to always keep that original intention at the forefront — so that when the team is making products, designing, and making every decision, it all unfolds around this core. And the soul of the company truly lands in every detail.
