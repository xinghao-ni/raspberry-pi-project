Key Neurochemical Finding

Individuals with ASD exhibit significantly lower total plasma oxytocin levels compared to age-matched controls, alongside an abnormally elevated ratio of oxytocin precursor (OT-X) to active oxytocin — indicating impaired synthesis, not simply lower production.

Low-stimulation, non-reciprocal interactions — such as observing a reptile — avoid triggering the cortisol stress response, creating conditions where natural oxytocin activity can function without suppression.

Beck & Clark, 2011 · Murry & Allen, 2012

Qualitative Research — Chapter 4.2 · Nantes 2025

Voices from the Field

Four semi-structured in-depth interviews were conducted in the Nantes region with a licensed reptile keeper, a family caregiver, design students, and a pet trade professional. Their accounts reveal convergent tensions between ethics, responsibility, and the latent therapeutic potential of non-conventional animal cohabitation.

01

Animal care is not merely a technical practice, but a fundamentally ethical and cultural responsibility. Human–animal coexistence must be grounded in deep respect for animal needs, natural behaviour, and environmental integrity.

Mickaël Grévois

Licensed Reptile Keeper · Welfare Advocate · Nantes

A multifaceted perspective on reptile keeping that interweaves therapeutic practice, legal regulation, and animal welfare ethics. Grévois distinguishes between responsible, welfare-oriented keepers and trend-driven, uninformed owners.

Reptile keeping requires long-term commitment, technical knowledge, and strict environmental control — not impulse acquisition.
Reptiles demonstrate therapeutic potential through calm, non-verbal interaction that supports emotional regulation in children with behavioural difficulties.
French and European legal frameworks are necessary safeguards against ecological disruption and animal welfare risks.
Ethics Legal Regulation Therapeutic Value Animal Welfare

Interview · §4.2.1 · Ni, X. (2025) · EDNA Nantes

02

Our turtles gradually shifted from being perceived as simple pets to becoming full members of the family. Animals are not decorative objects — they are sentient beings with emotional and physical needs.

Ayna

Family Caregiver · Aquatic Turtle Keeper · Nantes

A childhood experience of keeping aquatic turtles reveals how early human–animal interaction functions as informal education in responsibility, empathy, and care. Daily maintenance, behavioural observation, and health monitoring built deep emotional attachment.

Early co-habitation with reptiles teaches children curiosity, accountability, and long-term responsibility — without explicit instruction.
Turtles require complex environmental conditions: clean water, temperature regulation, sunlight, and adequate space — far from "low-maintenance."
Ethical companionship is grounded in genuine care, not control or convenience — a direct parallel to therapeutic animal interaction principles.
Family Care Childhood Experience Empathy Responsibility

Interview · §4.2.2 · Ni, X. (2025) · EDNA Nantes

03

Modern pet-keeping has become, to some extent, a symbolic social behaviour rather than a genuine response to animal needs. Pets increasingly become objects of display and aesthetic consumption.

Students from EDNA

Design Students · L'École de Design Nantes Atlantique

A generational perspective revealing how contemporary pet-keeping is often driven less by deliberate ethical reflection and more by habit, social inertia, and symbolic attachment. Urban lifestyle, limited living space, and industry aesthetics all shape these patterns.

Many people continue keeping pets as a normalised life practice, frequently without establishing clear boundaries or long-term responsibility.
Fast-paced urban lifestyles and restrictive regulations often make it difficult to meet animals' real needs, leading to neglect and abandonment.
The well-being of companion animals today is largely defined by how human societies choose to regulate, manage, and spatially accommodate them.
Urban Lifestyle Social Behaviour Pet Culture Ethics

Interview · §4.2.3 · Ni, X. (2025) · EDNA Nantes

04

Reptiles are chosen in cities because they are perceived as quiet and space-efficient — yet this convenience-driven logic often overlooks their emotional, environmental, and physiological needs.

Pet Shop Retailer

Animal Trade Professional · Reptile Specialist · Nantes

An industry-insider account revealing the contradictory landscape of reptile keeping shaped by urban living conditions, legal regulation, and ethical debate. Deep tensions between rescue intentions and ecological interference, between responsibility and impulsive ownership.

While some keepers demonstrate lifelong commitment and professional knowledge, others acquire large or exotic species for social display, leading to neglect.
France's particularly strict legal framework contrasts with more permissive systems in Europe — seen as a societal mechanism to control irresponsible behaviour.
Captivity frames as both a necessary tool for conservation and biomedical survival (antivenom production), and a profound ethical dilemma.

Service Design Framework

Staged Therapeutic Pathway

Phase 01 — Orientation

Environmental Familiarisation

Child and family enter the low-stimulation space. No direct animal contact. Ambient sound design at ≤40dB. Controlled lighting at 2700K warm. Visual introductions only — observation through transparent enclosure.

Phase 02 — Observation

Passive Proximity

Structured observation sessions of 15–20 minutes. Child controls distance and duration. Therapist narrates behaviour patterns in calm, slow cadence. Parent present throughout. Exit at any signal.

Phase 03 — Contact

Graduated Touch Interaction

Optional, child-initiated physical interaction. Structured rules about handling — both for child safety and animal welfare. Tactile input: smooth scales, slow temperature exchange, no sudden movement.

Phase 04 — Co-Regulation

Routine-Based Caregiving

Child participates in feeding and care rituals. Rule-based structure provides regulatory scaffold. Repetition and predictability support oxytocin system stabilisation. Progress documented for family review.

Phase 05 — Integration

Family & School Transfer

Generalisation of skills. Liaison with school inclusion coordinator. Parent coaching on low-stimulation environments at home. Optional home reptile introduction protocol provided.

Case Study: Tiffany

Tiffany
41 ans · ASD L1 · Nantes

Tiffany

Gymnastic Coach · Library Host · Mère de 3 filles · ASD Level 1

Tiffany is 41 years old, a rhythmic gymnastics coach and judge who also works as a library host welcoming school classes. She lives in a top-floor attic apartment in Nantes with her three daughters. She received her autism diagnosis after age 30 — years after experiencing a burnout triggered by her youngest daughter Savanna's birth and health complications.

Initially misdiagnosed with postpartum depression, she was referred to a neuropsychologist whose assessment revealed patterns consistent with ASD. After several weeks of in-depth testing, she received her diagnosis — learning she had been autistic her entire life without knowing it.

Her autism manifests primarily through hypersensitivity to textures, sound, and light. Touching a glass just out of the dishwasher can feel painful. Neon lighting in supermarkets sends too much information to her brain. Background white noise — ventilation systems, vacuum cleaners — accumulates invisibly until a small additional stimulus causes complete sensory freeze. She describes this through a "bucket" metaphor: stimuli fill up through the day until there is no more room.

Her directness and frank communication — a trait of her autism — had been misread as immaturity throughout her life. "On me pose une question, je réponds. Je vais pas réfléchir à tout l'aspect psychologique derrière." People perceived this as lack of empathy, when it was simply the absence of an automatic social filtering mechanism.

Life Challenges — Tiffany's Daily Reality

🧠
Sensory Overload Accumulation

Background noise (ventilation, appliances), flickering neon lights, and unexpected textures silently fill her sensory "bucket" throughout the day. By afternoon, even minor stimuli can cause complete freeze or panic.

🔄
Cognitive Rigidity & Transitions

Mental anticipation of specific outcomes makes flexibility extremely difficult. She may leave a bakery in tears if her intended item is unavailable — her brain cannot rapidly generate alternatives without pre-planning.

👥
Social Misreading & Masking

Decades of being labelled "immature" for direct communication. Her micro-expressions read as cold or arrogant to neurotypicals. Years of social masking depleted her energy and contributed directly to her burnout.

⚡
Undiagnosed Until Age 30+

Her burnout after Savanna's birth was the crisis that finally led to assessment. She had lived her entire childhood, adolescence, and early adult life without understanding the neurological reasons for her experiences.
